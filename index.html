The Channel Landscape
Every channel serves a different purpose and reaches people in a different mindset. The goal is to match the right message to the right environment.
Meta (Facebook / Instagram)
Reach small business owners where they already spend time. Carousel ads, video, retargeting. The foundation of any paid social strategy.
YouTube
Product demos, testimonials, educational content. Longer format lets us show the platform in action and build trust before the click.
TikTok
Quick, punchy content that catches attention fast. Behind-the-scenes, tips for business owners, product highlights that feel native to the platform.
Programmatic Display
Automated banner and display placements across the web. Keeps Deposyt visible to prospects as they browse industry-relevant sites and content.
Native Ads
Content that blends into the platform it lives on. Articles, sponsored posts, in-feed placements that add value instead of interrupting.
OOH / DOOH
Out-of-home and digital out-of-home. Billboards, transit, digital screens in high-traffic areas. Brand presence in the physical world where business owners operate.
Right message. Right place.
Right audience.
Tailored Creative, Not Repurposed Ads
Each channel has its own language, format and audience expectation. A TikTok that works won't work as a YouTube pre-roll. A Meta carousel shouldn't be copy-pasted into a native ad placement. Once we know which channels we're investing in, we tailor the creative specifically for each one so the communication feels organic and value-added instead of just another ad people scroll past.
The design system makes this scalable.
With the design language established, adapting creative for different channels becomes a formatting exercise rather than starting from scratch every time. The brand stays consistent, the message stays on point, and the team can move fast across all of them.
Know where they are. Meet them there properly.

Two Conversations, One Strategy
Deposyt's biggest strength is that it does two things: payment processing and business growth tools. But those two things appeal to people at different stages. Someone who's never heard of Deposyt cares about saving money on processing fees. Someone who's already on the platform needs to see how the tools solve their day-to-day problems. Trying to say both at once dilutes both messages. Splitting the funnel lets us talk to each audience on their terms.

Upper Funnel: The Payments Hook
For merchants who don't know Deposyt yet, the conversation starts with money. Lower processing fees, transparent pricing, no hidden costs. That's the hook that cuts through the noise because every business owner paying to accept payments wants to pay less. The targeting here is by merchant type: restaurants, retail, CBD, e-commerce, SaaS, service businesses. Each vertical has its own version of the payments pain point, and the creative should reflect that.
Restaurants
Tight margins, high volume. Lead with savings per transaction and how quickly it adds up across a month of service.
Retail / E-commerce
Online and in-store. Lead with seamless payment integration and the cost savings of consolidating under one provider.
CBD / High-Risk
Most processors won't touch them. Deposyt does. That alone is the hook. Reliability and acceptance where others say no.
Lower Funnel: The Tools Play
Once a merchant is on Deposyt or already aware of the brand, the message shifts entirely. Stop talking about payments and start talking about their business. The 200+ tools become the focus: calendars, funnels, invoicing, campaigns, analytics. The angle here is specific to each vendor's problems. A restaurant owner needs booking and scheduling. A service business needs invoicing and client management. The creative maps the tool to the problem and shows how Deposyt already solves it.
Problem-Led Messaging
Don't lead with the tool name. Lead with the headache it fixes. "Still chasing invoices?" hits harder than "Try our invoicing feature."
Tool-to-Vendor Mapping
Match specific tools to specific merchant types. A CBD shop doesn't need the same pitch as a hair salon. The more relevant, the higher the conversion.
Upsell Through Value
Merchants who came for payments discover they can run campaigns, manage clients and track analytics from the same dashboard.
One platform, two stories.
The upper funnel sells the financial win. The lower funnel sells the operational win. Together they move a merchant from "I save money here" to "I run my business here." What happens after that conversion is where the Retention strategy picks up.

Continued Value
Merchants stay when they feel like the platform is actively working for them. Not just processing their payments in the background, but showing up with useful information, timely nudges, and proof that Deposyt is saving them money and making their life easier.
01 Value Reporting. Regular updates that show merchants exactly how much they've saved, how many transactions they've processed, and how their usage has grown. Make the value visible so it's never taken for granted.
02 Expansion Triggers. When a merchant hits a milestone or their usage patterns suggest they'd benefit from another tool, surface it. Not as a sales pitch but as a recommendation that genuinely helps them.
03 Tool Walkthroughs and Education. Short, practical guides that help merchants get more out of tools they're already paying for. A merchant who uses three tools is stickier than one who uses one.
04 Check-in Emails. Not automated drip campaigns that feel robotic. Genuine touchpoints that ask how things are going, flag new features, or just remind them there's a real team behind the platform.
05 Holiday, Promo and Price Matching. Seasonal offers, loyalty discounts, and price matching that reward merchants for staying. Small gestures that signal Deposyt values the relationship, not just the transaction.
Community Based
Beyond the product itself, there's an opportunity to build something merchants actually want to be part of. A community layer that turns Deposyt from a tool into a network.
01 Private Merchant Community. A dedicated space (Facebook group, Reddit, custom forum) where merchants can ask questions, share wins, and get answers from moderators and each other. When someone feels part of a community, switching costs go beyond money.
02 Referral Program. Reward merchants who bring in other merchants. Turns your happiest customers into a sales channel and deepens their own investment in the brand.
03 Feedback Loop / Beta Access. Give engaged merchants early access to new tools and let them shape the product. Makes them feel invested in the platform's direction and builds advocates before a feature even launches.
04 Highlights / Content from Merchants. Spotlight real merchants using the platform. Their stories become marketing content and retention tools at the same time. A merchant who's been featured has a deeper connection to the brand than one who hasn't.
05 Merchant Events / Webinars. Live sessions that bring merchants together around topics that matter to them. Product walkthroughs, Q&As with the team, industry-specific discussions. Builds connection and gives Deposyt a human face beyond the dashboard.
Retention feeds back into growth.
A retained merchant refers others, creates content, joins the community, and adopts more tools over time. The targeting work brings them in, the retention work keeps them, and the two feed into each other. It's a loop, not a line.

Priorities
Before We Build, We Align
Everything in this whiteboard is possible but not everything should happen at once. Before any work kicks off, we need to sit down and get honest about what matters most right now. The answers to these questions shape the timeline, the resourcing and the order of operations.
What are the current needs?
What's the most pressing thing the business needs from marketing right now? Is it more leads, better brand awareness, stronger vendor relationships, or something else entirely? This is the anchor that everything else gets prioritised against.
What metrics do we want to hit?
Are we optimising for conversion, retention, or both? What does success look like in 3 months, 6 months, a year? Defining the numbers early means we can measure progress instead of guessing whether things are working.
Do we audit current marketing performance first?
There's already money being spent on Google Ads and content being posted on social. Before launching anything new, do we want a full audit of what's performing, what's underperforming, and where spend is being wasted? That baseline tells us where the quick wins are.
Do we develop branding and messaging first?
If the design language and voice aren't locked in, everything that gets produced will need to be redone later. There's a case for getting the foundation right before scaling output. It's slower upfront but saves time and money long term.
Do we need a better understanding of the market?
How well do we know who the current customers actually are and where the untapped opportunities sit? If the audience data is thin, investing in research early will make every campaign that follows significantly more effective.
Build out a cohesive voice and look across all socials?
Social is the most visible channel Deposyt has. If the priority is to get the public-facing presence sharp and consistent first, that becomes the starting point. A unified social presence builds credibility fast and gives the team something tangible to point to early on.
The answers shape everything.
Timeline. Budget. Order of operations.
Timeline
The timeline depends entirely on which priorities come first. A branding and design system sprint could take 4 to 6 weeks. A full marketing audit might take 2 to 3. Launching campaigns on new channels needs the foundation work done first. The honest conversation about what matters most is what builds a realistic timeline that the team can actually deliver against.
Resourcing and Budget
Once the priorities are set, we can map what's needed to deliver them. That includes team capacity (covered in the Team Breakdown), tool and platform costs, ad spend allocation, and any external support. The budget should follow the priorities, not the other way around. Start lean, prove the approach works, and scale the investment as results come in.
This is a conversation, not a prescription.
These are jump-off points for where a plan and conversation can begin with the existing and new team. Getting everyone aligned, setting clear goal markers, and making sure expectations are out in the open so there's no confusion or surprises down the line.

Conversion
The Real Barrier Isn't Price
One of the biggest pain points heard directly from vendors is how complex these systems are and how switching between them feels like an enormous task, even when the alternative is cheaper and better. People are staying with platforms that don't work for them, paying more than they should, simply because the hassle of migrating feels worse than the status quo.
The Switching Problem
It's not that merchants don't want to switch. Many are already on the fence. They've seen better options, done the maths on fees, and know they're overpaying. But the thought of moving their entire operation, learning a new dashboard, re-entering their data, re-training their staff, that's what keeps them where they are. The friction of change outweighs the frustration of staying.
Make switching feel like
the easiest decision they've made.
The Opportunity
If Deposyt can build a genuinely smooth transition process and wrap a campaign around it, there's a real opening. We're not just competing on features or price at that point. We're removing the one thing that's actually stopping people from moving. The campaign writes itself: "We handle the switch. You just show up."
Smooth the Process
Dedicated onboarding support, data migration assistance, step-by-step walkthroughs. Make the switch feel guided, not overwhelming.
Sweeten the Offer
Limited-time switching incentives, fee-free trial periods, price matching. Give people on the fence a reason to move now instead of later.
Let Switchers Speak
Merchants who already made the switch are the most powerful proof. Their stories answer every objection before it's even raised.
Campaign Angles
01 "We handle the switch" campaigns that lead with the ease of transition, not just the product. Address the fear directly.
02 Side-by-side comparisons showing what merchants are paying now vs what they'd pay with Deposyt. Make the cost of staying visible.
03 Switcher testimonials from real merchants who made the move. "I wish I'd done it sooner" is the most persuasive line in marketing.
04 Time-limited offers paired with the transition support. "Switch this month, we'll move everything for you and waive fees for 90 days."
The product already wins on value. The gap is in making the move feel safe.
If the transition experience becomes part of the brand promise, Deposyt doesn't just compete with other platforms on features. It competes on the thing nobody else is talking about: how painless it is to get started.

The copywriter refines and sharpens rather than starting from a blank page every time. Faster output, consistent tone, more time for the writing that actually needs a human touch.
Workflow Automation
Connect the pipeline end to end. Briefs generate copy docs, copy docs feed into creative builders, finished assets get tagged and stored automatically, and deployment to channels can be scheduled or triggered. Less manual handoff, fewer things falling through the cracks, faster time from idea to live.
Website and Bespoke Solutions
AI tools can help update website content, build landing pages, generate product descriptions, and create bespoke solutions like interactive tools or calculators that would normally require dedicated development time. The team stays lean but the output doesn't look like it.
The Human Stays in the Loop
AI handles the volume. Humans handle the judgement. Every piece of output still gets reviewed, refined and approved by the team before it goes live. The point isn't to remove people from the process, it's to remove the busywork so they can focus on the decisions that actually matter: is this on-brand, does this land, does this serve the goal?
The multiplier effect.
A five-person team with AI integrated into their workflow can produce, test, and iterate at a pace that used to require three times the headcount. The design system gives AI the guardrails. The team gives it direction. Together they move fast without cutting corners.

Testing, Metrics and Iterations
Measure Everything, Guess Nothing
Creative decisions should be informed by data, not just instinct. Working closely with social media managers and the wider team, we monitor how every piece of content performs across channels. What's getting engagement, what's driving clicks, what's falling flat. That feedback loop is what turns a good campaign into a great one.
Learning What Works and Why
It's not enough to know that something performed well. We need to understand why. Was it the format, the hook, the time of day, the audience segment? Breaking down wins and losses at that level gives us a playbook that gets sharper with every campaign.
The work doesn't end
when it goes live.
That's when it starts talking back.
Testing with Purpose
A/B testing headlines, creative formats, CTAs, audience segments. Not testing for the sake of it, but running structured experiments that answer specific questions. Does a testimonial-led ad outperform a feature-led one? Does short copy convert better than long? Each test sharpens the next round of work.
Creative Testing
Different visuals, formats, and hooks tested against each other to find what resonates with each audience segment.
Copy Testing
Headlines, CTAs, tone of voice, long vs short. Finding the language that moves people from scrolling to clicking.
Channel Testing
Same campaign adapted for different platforms to see where Deposyt's message lands hardest and where budget goes furthest.
Metrics That Guide the Creative
Tracking isn't a reporting exercise. The numbers feed directly back into the creative process. If video outperforms static, we make more video. If a specific merchant segment responds to testimonial content, we double down on it. The metrics become the brief for the next round of work.
What We Track
▶ Engagement rates across platforms
▶ Click-through and conversion rates by campaign
▶ Cost per acquisition by channel
▶ Content format performance (video vs static vs carousel)
▶ Retention and churn signals from existing merchants
How It Feeds Back
▶ Monthly creative reviews informed by performance data
▶ Winning patterns folded into the design system and templates
▶ Underperformers analysed and iterated on, not just scrapped
▶ Trend awareness baked into the process so the brand stays current
▶ Quarterly strategy adjustments based on cumulative learnings
The brand never sits still.
The most important part of this process is that learnings fold back into the creative. The design system evolves, the messaging sharpens, the targeting gets tighter. Every campaign makes the next one better. That's how a lean team stays ahead of the curve.

Creative Collaboration
Open Process, Open Doors
With a small team, there's no reason for work to happen behind closed doors. Everyone sees the brief, everyone sees the work in progress, everyone has a chance to weigh in before things go out. That openness is what makes a lean team fast. Fewer handoffs, fewer surprises, and decisions that don't need three rounds of approval because the right people were in the room from the start.
Taste Isn't a Title
You don't need "Creative Director" in your title to know what looks right, sounds right, or feels off. Everyone on this team brings a perspective shaped by their craft, their experience and their instincts. The copywriter might catch a visual issue. The motion designer might suggest a better headline. That cross-pollination is where the best work comes from, and a team this size makes it easy to tap into.
Small team. Big opinions.
That's the advantage.
Working in Cadence
When the whole team moves through projects together, nobody falls out of the loop. Everyone understands the campaigns, the goals and the context behind each piece of work. That shared understanding means anyone can jump in and support where needed without a two-hour catch-up. It also means the work stays cohesive because everyone's pulling in the same direction.

▶ Quick creative reviews where feedback flows both ways
▶ Concepts shaped by the group, not handed down by one person
▶ Campaign retrospectives that capture what worked and what to try next
Why It Works at This Stage

▶ Processes built here become the playbook as the team scales
▶ New hires inherit a way of working, not chaos
Built to form. Built to grow.
This collaborative model works now because the team is small and the foundation is being laid. But the expertise, the standards and the culture that come out of this stage are exactly what allow the team to scale without losing what makes it work. The habits set here carry forward.

Why This Exists
Everything in this whiteboard is strategy. This section is what it looks like when strategy gets applied. It walks through what exists today, where the gaps are, and what changes when the brand system is used properly.
The Current State: Product Offering
The existing landing page has a strong headline: "We Put A Percentage Back In Your Pocket" and a clear value proposition around zero credit card fees. The intent is solid. It's trying to communicate savings, simplicity, and the all-in-one nature of the platform.
Reference: Current Product Offering Page
The visual execution doesn't reinforce the message though. The hero image is a generic phone and card mockup surrounded by floating icons: percentage signs, dollar signs, abstract shapes. These elements are decorative, not informative. They don't show the product doing anything. A user scrolling past this sees "fintech landing page," not "here's what Deposyt does for you." The brand isn't being reinforced through colour or through messaging. There's no purple, no gradient, no Deposyt identity in the imagery at all.
01 The imagery is generic. A phone mockup with an "Approved" screen, a credit card, and scattered icons. It could belong to any payment company. There's nothing uniquely Deposyt about it.
02 The visual doesn't prove the claim. The copy says "zero credit card fees" but the image shows a standard transaction approval screen. There's no connection between what's being promised and what's being shown.
03 Brand colours are absent. The page is almost entirely black and white with blue accents. Deposyt's purple gradient palette, the thing that should make the brand instantly recognisable, doesn't appear anywhere.
04 No immediate value demonstration. The "Sell $100, Receive $100" line is buried below the fold. The visual should be doing this job on its own, without the user needing to read further.
The message is there. The visual
isn't selling it.
The Redesign: Meta Ad Concept
Same core message, run through Deposyt's design language. Same product. Same value proposition. Completely different result.
This is a redesigned layout for a Meta feed ad. It takes the same "zero credit card fees" message and makes it impossible to miss. Instead of decorative icons, the visual is a product UI showing a fee breakdown: processing fees, transaction fees, monthly charges, all crossed out, landing on $0 with Deposyt. The image does the selling. The brand colours are front and centre, the messaging is direct, and the visual actually reinforces the claim rather than sitting beside it.
01 Brand colours lead. Deep purple background, gradient accents. This is unmistakably Deposyt. Even at thumbnail size in a crowded feed, it stands out and builds recognition over time.
02 The UI proves the claim. A mock fee summary shows real fee types being eliminated. The user doesn't have to imagine the savings. They can see them itemised and struck through, landing at $0.
03 Clear, decisive messaging. "All-In-One Merchant Solution" as the category label. "Zero Credit Card Fees" as the headline. No ambiguity. The value is immediate.
04 Visual hierarchy that works at scale. Logo top-left for brand anchor. Product UI in the centre for proof. Headline at the bottom for the close. The eye moves naturally from identity to evidence to action.
Same message. Designed to
actually land.
